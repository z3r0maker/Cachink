Xangarro Postgres: round 3, auditing the optimizations

2026-09-26 · branch perf/db-scale @ bfc8a6d7 (after the round-2 fixes) · previous rounds: db-2026-09-17.md, db-2026-09-26.html

Verdict. The optimizations hold up. The batched push is correct on every main path. Seqs come out in delta order and in commit order, last-write-wins works row by row, a re-push is idempotent, a foreign id is isolated by splitting the batch, and no push overflows the subtransaction cache. It measured again at 47–67 ms per 500 rows. Across 203 tenants on an 8 GB dataset, every portal read at month scale has a p95 under 70 ms. The new indexes cost storage (+23%), but no measurable write throughput.

A second pass over the new code and what it now makes possible found 31 new findings: 7 high (2 of them critical), 13 medium, 11 low. Two of them would stop a business from working at all.

  1. A phone or caja cannot be linked once a heavy tenant has about a month of stock movements. The bootstrap sends every movement ever recorded in one response, and Vercel caps a response at 4.5 MB (DB3-BOOT-01).
  2. One bad row can stop a device syncing, forever. Examples: a NUL character in a text field, or a batch the server refuses as a whole (DB3-SYNC-01).

Two more lose or hide data on the browser caja: two open tabs overwrite each other's unsent sales, and cierre counts the wrong rows. One export and one Estados range can exhaust a Vercel function's memory. Finally, two of the round-2 migrations need editing before they reach the hosted database, or they will fail or briefly stall tables during the day.

1. The 17:00–20:00 peak, remodelled

The model uses the code's real parameters:

  • push debounce 2 s; jittered 12–18 min phone tick; caja pull at most every 45 s
  • 500 rows per push; 5,000 entries per pull page
  • 2 connections per Vercel instance; a Supabase Nano pooler with about 15 server connections for the app role (an assumption)
  • the busiest 15 minutes run at 3× the window's average

A 10-minute outage at 20:00, 5,000 tenants.

  • The good news: the new client backoff works. Automatic triggers wait, and pulls are skipped while pushes fail.
  • At the start of the outage: retries peak at about 310 req/s in the first 1–2 minutes. That is 2.8× normal and above what the pool serves at a 3 ms round trip.
  • During it: if it's the database that's down, every request waits the 10 s connect timeout. That is about 3,000 Vercel functions running at once.
  • A gap in the backoff: cashiers pressing «Reintentar envío» skip it, and a 5xx carries no Retry-After.
  • At recovery: about +66 req/s for roughly 5 minutes, within the pool.
  • The fix (DB3-SYNC-05): answer 503 with Retry-After when getting a connection takes more than about 2 s.

Payload sizes:

  • A 500-row push is about 220–285 KB, which is fine.
  • A 5,000-entry pull page is about 2.1 MB of movements. A page of product changes would cross 4.5 MB if atributos averages more than about 250 B.
  • The bootstrap: see DB3-BOOT-01.

2. Findings

Sources: R1 = push and sync server, R2 = migrations and schema, R3 = portal reads, R4 = device client and peak model. "Mine" = measured on the 8 GB scale dataset. Every high finding was reproduced or measured, not just inferred.

High: fix before launch

criticalDB3-BOOT-01After about a month of stock movements, a heavy tenant can no longer link a phone or caja
Evidence
  • server/device/bootstrap.ts:58 selects every live inventory_movements row for activation and for since=0, as one JSON body with no paging.
  • Measured: about 400–425 B per movement, so 4.5 MB is about 11K movements. That is roughly 1 month for a heavy tenant (10K tickets/month), 3.4 months for a mid one, 10 days at the 30K cap.
  • On the scale dataset: a heavy tenant at 1 month was 4.6 MB; the one-year whale was 159 MB.
  • The caja also discards bootstrap.serverSeq when it links (acceso/vincular.tsx:92-103), so its first pull is a second full bootstrap.
  • Activation parses the bootstrap with zod while holding businesses FOR UPDATE.
  • On the device, forgetEchoes builds an IN (…) list that goes past SQLite's 32,766-variable limit at about 33K movements.
Fix

This is round 2's open DB2-QRY-05, now confirmed to break activation:

  1. Bootstrap from a per-product stock baseline plus recent movements, paged through the seq stream (at most 5,000 rows or 2 MB per page). This amends ADR-081 and the pull contract.
  2. Store the activation's serverSeq on the caja.
  3. Do the zod parse and the signing outside the transaction.
  4. Chunk forgetEchoes.

R1-05, R4-01, R4-06, mine
criticalDB3-SYNC-01One bad row or batch can stop a device syncing forever
Evidence
  • (a) A NUL character. The row write fails (22021) and is isolated as INTERNAL. Then saveRejections casts its payload ::jsonb, which fails (22P05) outside any savepoint. The transaction rolls back and the server answers 500. The phone keeps its cursor and sends the same batch forever. Reproduced: 3 good rows plus 1 with 'Refresco\u0000' → 0 rows stored.
  • (b) A batch refused as a whole. A 400 (stricter schema against an old app or caja tab), a 413 (unbounded products.atributos) or a deterministic 500 leaves the outbox cursor where it was, so every later capture queues behind it. Reproduced with two drains → 400, cursor unchanged.
  • (c) Deterministic errors reported as retryable. store-error.ts:45-49 maps every non-transient error to INTERNAL (retryable). That includes 23505 on idx_tickets_device_folio and 22xxx data errors, so they are retried forever.
Fix
  • Refuse NUL (and lone surrogates) in the contract, as a terminal VALIDATION.
  • Map 22021/22P05/22P02 and non-id 23505 to terminal codes.
  • Run reject in a savepoint, with a fallback that stores the rejection without its payload.
  • On the client, on a 400/413 (or N identical 5xx), halve the batch down to one row, mark that row rejected locally, and advance.
  • Cap atributos.

Evidence

highDB3-CAJA-02The cierre gate and the pill count the wrong rows
Evidence
They count only status='pending'.
  • Sales captured offline have no status row, so they count as 0 and the cashier can close with unsent sales.
  • Rows from one failed push become pending and block cierre until the network comes back, against ADR-053 §5.
  • Before the first flush after a page load, the gate shows the fixture's 3 phantom records.
  • The phone counts pending + retrying, so the two surfaces disagree.

Reproduced: 3 offline sales → pending: 0; one failed push → pending: 6.

Fix
One unsentCount() in StatusStore (change-log entries above the push high-water mark + pending + rejected), used on both surfaces. Decide the cierre rule by ADR; that is owner decision 1 in DS-06.

highDB3-EXP-01Exports are built in memory: a one-year whale needs 2.7 GB
Evidence

R3-02
highDB3-MIG-01Two round-2 migrations must be edited before they reach the hosted database
Evidence
  • 0043: the concurrent builds fail on a live database. It sets lock_timeout = 3s, so each CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY fails whenever any transaction in the database holds a snapshot for more than 3 s (metering has a 60 s timeout, admin 15 s). Each failure leaves an INVALID index behind: writes still maintain it, and a unique one still enforces uniqueness. Reproduced both ways: an open writer, and a snapshot held on a different table.
  • 0045 and 0044: they stall traffic while they wait. ALTER POLICY takes ACCESS EXCLUSIVE on 29 tables, including sales, sync_log and devices. While it waits up to 3 s for the lock, every reader queues behind it. Reproduced: a plain count(*) on sales stalled for 2.5 s. 0044's ALTER TABLE on api_latency_counters does the same.
Fix
  • In 0043, use SET lock_timeout = 0 (or several minutes) before the CONCURRENTLY statements. They only take SHARE UPDATE EXCLUSIVE, so a long wait harms nobody.
  • For ACCESS EXCLUSIVE DDL, use 100–250 ms with a retry-and-backoff loop in the runner, and run it in the 00:00–06:00 trough.
  • Teach lint.ts both rules.

Safe to edit now: none of these files is in the hosted ledger yet.

Medium

mediumDB3-QRY-01Excluding cancelled tickets scans every ticket the tenant has, and can flip to 19.5 s

The NOT EXISTS (tickets … cancelled_at IS NOT NULL) in totalsForRange (Inicio ×2, metas ×2), serieDiaria, periodLedger and the Asesor (×2) hashes all of the tenant's tickets. It takes 77 ms on the whale and grows with years.

Measured: after a burst of cancellations, before autovacuum refreshed the statistics, the same month query flipped to a nested loop and took 19,565 ms, past the 5 s timeout. With a partial index tickets (business_id, id) WHERE cancelled_at IS NOT NULL it takes 45 ms whatever the history.

Fix: add that index CONCURRENTLY, plus the per-table analyze settings in DB3-OPS-01. (R2-05, R3-05, mine)

mediumDB3-SYNC-02A transient failure on a product or client permanently rejects its children

If a HYBRID product or client in a push comes back INTERNAL, its sales and movements in the same push come back FK_*_MISSING, which is terminal. The batching widens this: a lock timeout now fails the whole products write, and the savepoint-budget spill makes innocent products INTERNAL.

Reproduced: a product INTERNAL left its movement terminally rejected. In memory, 67 bad products out of 200 left 123 sales terminally rejected.

Fix: answer INTERNAL (retryable) when the referenced row appears earlier in the same push. (R1-02, R4-08)

mediumDB3-SYNC-03A retry that overlaps its original push gets terminal rejections for rows that were stored

Receipts are read at the start of a segment. A retry whose insert waited on the original's row lock then sees exists with no receipt, and answers DUPLICATE_CONFLICT (plus FK_*_MISSING for dependent rows).

Reproduced: the original push accepted its rows, and the overlapping retry got 2 false terminal rejections. The phone's 30 s timeout under a slow pool (DB3-SYNC-05) or two tabs (DB3-CAJA-01) triggers it.

Fix: for rows that come back exists or stale without a receipt, look the receipts up again after the write, and count them as written for references. (R1-03, R4-07)

mediumDB3-SYNC-04INTERNAL rejections stay open after the retry succeeds

accept never resolves earlier rejections for the same row, so the portal badge and the staff digest keep counting them. Batching produces more INTERNAL rows than before.

Fix: one UPDATE sync_rejections SET resolved_at = now() for the accepted keys, pipelined with the receipts. (R1-04)

mediumDB3-SYNC-05No bound on waiting for a pool connection, and no load shedding

postgres.js queues without an acquire timeout. With 2 connections per instance:

  • A slow holder (export, Asesor, a yearly Estados) stalls every other request on the same instance.
  • A push can outlive the phone's 30 s timeout, and that is exactly what triggers DB3-SYNC-03.
  • A database outage turns into thousands of in-flight functions (§1).

There is no deadlock: no path holds a connection while waiting for another.

Fix:

  • Race the tenant transaction against a 10–15 s timer, and answer 503 with Retry-After.
  • Give exports their own pool.
  • Fold the device-auth read into the push transaction. Today it is 4 round trips and 3 separate connection checkouts.

(R1-06, R3-17, R4 outage model)

mediumDB3-CAJA-03An idle caja never pulls, so a deactivated operator's NIP keeps working

The caja pulls only after a capture, on the online event, or on a manual retry. There is no timer and no pull on boot. NIPs are checked locally, so a deactivated operator's NIP works on an idle caja until the next sale, and that sale uses the old prices.

Fix: pull every 5 min ±20% while the tab is visible, on visibilitychange and on boot, behind the same backoff. (R4-05)

mediumDB3-CAJA-04The caja rewrites its whole database after every sale, and the database only grows

It runs sql.export() plus a full OPFS file rewrite twice per sale. Pulled rows are never purged. The heavy-tenant estimate after a year is 40–60 MB, about 150 MB of transient memory, and hundreds of ms per sale, a risk on an iPad (speculative). Two overlapping writes can also finish out of order.

Fix: serialise and debounce the writes, then move to an OPFS VFS (wa-sqlite or the official sqlite-wasm), which needs an ADR-071 decision. Fold old pulled movements into a stock baseline. (R4-10, round-2 note)

mediumDB3-QRY-02The Asesor reads full history on every GET, and the GET also writes

It takes 3.35 s on the whale. primer_dia is a hash anti-join over all lines (339 ms; 28 ms as ORDER BY … LIMIT 1). The page is about 16 transactions, and a GET upserts insights. At 5 years on Nano it passes the 5 s timeout.

Fix: compute in the existing cron and make the page read-only; cap the counts at their thresholds. (R3-04)

mediumDB3-QRY-03The Movimientos summary, paging and search still grow with range and history
  • Summary: resumenMovimientos joins all of the tenant's tickets and spills count(DISTINCT) to disk: month 82–124 ms, year 690 ms, open range 890 ms, about 5 s at 5 years. It runs again on every page flip.
  • Paging: OFFSET reaches 163–260 ms on deep pages, and pages shift when rows are inserted.
  • Search: ILIKE has no index (295 ms per statement over full history, run 3 times).

Fix: pre-aggregate sales per ticket, then join tickets over the same date range (tested 124 → 42 ms). Use keyset pages; the index already exists. Add a pg_trgm index on concepto, or require a period for search, which matches DS-01's hint. (R3-06, R3-09, R3-10)

mediumDB3-QRY-04Screens can disagree for the same period
  • ticketsConTotal (estados.ts) uses between, not fechaEnDias, keeps deleted tickets, and runs in a second transaction.
  • Sale lines whose ticket was rejected are orphans. ticketId isn't in PUSH_REFERENCES, so Inicio and Estados count those lines but Movimientos drops them. Tested: a $999 gap.
  • Deleted lines inflate fiado in Revisión and Cortes.
  • 2026-02-30 passes validation and rolls over into March.
  • Portal stock movements are stamped with the UTC date, which is tomorrow between 18:00 and 24:00 CDMX, right in the peak window.

(R3-03, R3-07, R3-13, R3-14, R3-15)

mediumDB3-IDX-01The index set needs a second pass

See §3. In short:

  • Drop sales_business_idx and expenses_business_idx. The live partial indexes serve every date query, and on sales the current set doubled insert time (1.20 → 2.43 s per 300K rows).
  • Drop business_members_business_idx, a prefix of the new unique index.

(R2-03/04/06/07/18, mine)

mediumDB3-OPS-01No per-table storage or autovacuum settings anywhere

reloptions are empty. The index-only scans 0043 relies on showed heap fetches only until a VACUUM ran, and stale statistics produced DB3-QRY-01's 19.5 s plan flip.

Fix:

  • Fillfactor 70–80 on the hot-update rows: devices, sync_cursors, throttle, api_latency_counters, portal_sessions.
  • On the append-heavy tables (sales, tickets, expenses, inventory_movements, sync_log, sync_receipts): autovacuum_vacuum_insert_scale_factor ≈ 0.01–0.02 and autovacuum_analyze_scale_factor ≈ 0.02.
  • Batch security_prune for its first run.

Measured as fine: devices and sync_cursors stayed over 99% HOT under 42K updates. (R2-17, R2-19, mine)

mediumDB3-SEC-01SECURITY DEFINER functions search public before pg_catalog

negocios_for_user, memberships_for_user, redeem_* and tenant_plan_* use search_path = public, pg_catalog, and verify.ts accepts that as pinned. negocios_for_user also returns any user's memberships for whatever id the app role passes; it isn't bound to the session.

Fix: SET search_path = pg_catalog (the bodies are already fully qualified), and derive the user from the session token hash. Hand this to the separate security audit, together with the device_id attribution note from round 2. (R2-09)

4. Verified correct

  • Batched push.
    • Seqs are consecutive in delta order across the products-first writes and across segments. The cursor lock lasts until commit. In a one-segment push it's taken only for the last 4 round trips.
    • Last-write-wins works row by row in the multi-row upsert. Grouping rows by shape never nulls a field a row leaves out.
    • A re-push is answered from receipts at millisecond precision. One foreign id is found by splitting, and a newer foreign row is answered DUPLICATE_CONFLICT without an error.
    • The savepoint budget (60 + 1) stays under the 64-entry cache.
    • Throughput: 47–67 ms per 500 rows, 20.8K rows/s with 15 clients, and 3 devices of one shop finish in 114 ms together.
  • Pull. It reads the cursor before the tables, and a truncated page's cursor is its last entry, so there are no gaps. The metering read is started with .catch attached, so no unhandled rejection. No request holds a connection while waiting for another, so a 2-connection pool can't deadlock.
  • Device client.
    • The cursor advances only after every row's outcome is recorded, and a lost response is answered from receipts.
    • Stale-pending recovery can't re-send a batch that is still in flight within one engine.
    • A batch never exceeds 500 rows.
    • No hot loops. The backoff math stays within its bounds, and the fetch timeout covers reading the body.
    • Rows the device just received aren't pushed back. The retention purge deletes only acknowledged rows.
  • Migrations.
    • 0043's duplicate guard refuses before building. The INVALID-index sweep plus IF NOT EXISTS make re-runs idempotent.
    • 0044 doesn't rewrite the table.
    • 0045 leaves 0 unwrapped policies, and every ALL policy has WITH CHECK.
    • 0046's CTE always touches and always returns the row.
    • Admin 0020–0022 are correct.
    • The runner selftest passes 10/10.
    • Role settings (JIT, timeouts) are in place.
  • Portal reads.
    • fechaEnDias is inclusive at both ends and handles the sentinel and timestamped values.
    • Cancelled and deleted rows are excluded consistently across the totals, the daily series, the ledger and the summary, apart from the orphan lines in DB3-QRY-04.
    • ILIKE escaping works, and the export keyset has no ties or gaps.
    • cache() is request-scoped, so no tenant data leaks between requests.
    • historialSync's seq/created_at assumption holds, because both are stamped under the cursor lock.
    • Month-scale screens: p95 under 70 ms across 203 tenants.
  • Hot rows. devices and sync_cursors stayed over 99% HOT under 42K updates, and autovacuum kept up.

6. The Azure note (O-32)

The note is on the branch as owner row O-32 in docs/plan/11-pre-launch-and-deferred.md. It appears in PENDIENTES.md. This round adds three sizing facts to it:

  1. Size the peak on connections, not CPU. 5,000 realistic tenants need about 3–6 busy connections at a 1–3 ms round trip. That is 17–41% of a Nano-class pooler. At the 30K cap it is 30–72, which is 2–5× over.
  2. Shed load with 503 + Retry-After instead of queueing, so an outage at 20:00 doesn't turn into thousands of waiting functions.
  3. Autovacuum and analyze need headroom at the peak, or plans flip (DB3-QRY-01). Schedule manual VACUUM/ANALYZE and any lock-taking DDL in the 00:00–06:00 trough.

7. Method

  • Review: four read-only reviewers (R1–R4) covered the full origin/main…perf/db-scale diff by area, each on a private Postgres container. Every high finding was reproduced on real Postgres, or measured.
